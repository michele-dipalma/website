The rise of antibiotic resistance poses a grave threat to global public health, creating an urgent need for new antibiotics. The natural antibiotic albicidin and its oligoarylamide analogues have shown great promise and are currently under preclinical investigation. However, they are effectively neutralised in Klebsiella pneumoniae by AlbA (80 kDa), a ligand-induced, self- upregulating dimeric MerR-family transcription factor, through sequestration by its drug-binding domain (AlbAS).
Although the albicidin–AlbAS interaction has been characterised at the molecular level, the mechanism by which antibiotic binding triggers transcriptional activation remains elusive and might hinder the development of this new class of compounds. To investigate this, we solved the X-ray crystal structures of AlbAS in complex with several synthetic pyrrolobenzodiazepine (PBD) antibiotics—whose resistance is also mediated by AlbA. We also determined the cryo- EM structures of the dimeric arrangement of AlbAS domains within full-length AlbA at 3.0 Å of resolution, both in the presence and absence of a pyrrolobenzodiazepine antibiotic, as well as the ternary complex between full-length AlbA, its promoter DNA and the bacterial RNA polymerase at 2.4 Å resolution. Our data suggest that albicidin binding induces activation through displacement of the conserved L141 residue from a hydrophobic pocket, leading to a major rearrangement of the AlbAS domains and a transition from an inactive to an active state. Binding of active full-length AlbA to its suboptimal promoter mediates DNA kinking and recognition by the σ70 subunit, initiating transcription and overexpression of its own gene and likely other antibiotic resistance genes. Furthermore, we show that in full-length AlbA, the pyrrolobenzodiazepine KMR-14-14 binds to AlbAS without interacting with L141, thereby avoiding activation as confirmed by an in vitro transcription assay.
These findings provide new molecular insights into AlbA-mediated antibiotic resistance and suggest that pyrrolobenzodiazepines could be employed as AlbA inhibitors to support oligoarylamide antibiotics in their clinical development against resistant priority pathogens.
