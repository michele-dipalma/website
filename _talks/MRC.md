Precise control of protein activity is essential in both eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells. One widespread regulatory strategy is autoinhibition, in which proteins adopt inactive conformations that must be relieved to enable function.
In this talk, structural insights into autoinhibition in two distinct systems will be presented. First, the motor protein kinesin-1 will be discussed, focusing on recent cryo-EM structures of a microtubule-bound, stalkless motor–tail chimera that provide further insights into the role of the IAK inhibitory motif in the stabilisation of the kinesin double-lockdown autoinhibited state. Then, X-ray crystallography and cryo-EM data on the bacterial antibiotic-sensing transcription factor AlbA will be analysed, illustrating how autoinhibition in this system is mechanistically relieved by antibiotic binding to regulate transcription of resistance genes.
Together, these results highlight how autoinhibition is implemented through distinct structural mechanisms to control protein function across diverse biological contexts.
